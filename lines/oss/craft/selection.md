# Selection

Depth beats filling slots. Prefer maintainer follow-ups on merged work, endorsed unclaimed issues, then unchecked tracking goals. Each unit needs an objective done-check and a user-visible benefit. TypeScript/React/Node tooling and frontend/full-stack work fit best; other languages are fine when valuable and locally verifiable.

Use health/policy to inspect outsider acceptance, review latency, backlog and fatigue over a stated 30–60 day window; read 3–5 closed outsider attempts, not just stars or maintainer merge counts. A flood_index of 0.9 or above means propose or skip. Follow the rotation, cooldown, exploration prerequisites and capacity in the current selection policy, not historical recommendations in cards.

Look for invariant breaks from supported real usage rather than racing fresh reports. Prefer clear conformance/parity goals, genuine performance gains and modules where prior accepted work gives context. A report younger than 48 hours is out unless a maintainer invited a PR. Recent claims (about 30 days), assignment, competing fork/open/merged PRs or two failed outsider attempts need rejection/qualification, not optimism.

A long-shipped behavior with a documented workaround may be deliberate. Propose a design question rather than labeling it a regression. Do not add docs/typo/cosmetic padding or target a line count. Zero worthwhile candidates is a successful scout.
